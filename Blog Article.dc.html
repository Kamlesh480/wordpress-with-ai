CHOOSING THE RIGHT NP GRADE FOR RCC HUME PIPES

RP
By Ramesh Patidar Published Sep 5, 2026 · Updated Sep 6, 2026 · 9 min read
RCC hume pipes stacked by NP grade at the casting yard, hero photograph
TL;DR

NP2 suits light footpath drainage, NP3 covers most municipal sewer lines, and NP4 is for highway crossings with deep cover. Grade decides wall thickness and load rating — confirm it before you order, not after the trench is dug.

Why Grade Selection Matters

Every RCC hume pipe we cast is classed NP2, NP3 or NP4 under IS 458. The grade decides wall thickness and reinforcement — get it wrong and a line either costs more than it needs to, or fails under a load it was never built for.

  • NP2: footpaths and light landscaping drainage with shallow cover and no vehicular load.
  • NP3: standard municipal stormwater and sewer lines under normal road cover.
  • NP4: highway crossings and deep cover where heavy vehicular load bears directly overhead.

Jointing a Pipe Run: Step by Step

  1. 1

    Inspect the socket and spigot

    ~5 MIN

    Check both ends for chips or hairline cracks before the pipe goes anywhere near the trench.

  2. 2

    Apply jointing lubricant

    ~5 MIN

    An even coat on the rubber ring — too little and the seal drags, too much and it can roll out of place.

  3. 3

    Align and draw the joint home

    ~15 MIN

    Pull the spigot fully into the socket with a come-along, checking gradient hasn't shifted.

  4. 4

    Pressure-check the seal

    ~20 MIN

    Hold the line under test pressure before backfill — this is the step most often skipped, and shouldn't be.

Site Safety Notes

Informational: pipes over 900mm diameter need two-point lifting slings, not a single chain.

Caution: do not enter an unshored trench deeper than 1.5m without edge protection.

Critical: never pressure-test a joint with workers standing over the trench line.

NP3 vs. NP4: Choosing by Cover Depth, Not Habit

Most contractors default to NP3 out of habit, and most of the time that's the right call — it covers standard municipal sewer and stormwater runs under normal road cover without paying for capacity nobody needs. The decision only gets interesting past about three metres of cover, where the soil load on top of the pipe starts to exceed what an NP3 wall was designed to carry.

NP4 earns its higher cost on highway crossings and any run where heavy vehicular load bears directly overhead with limited cover to spread that load first. We'd rather quote the heavier grade on a borderline site than have a contractor call us back after a crack shows up eighteen months into service — the price difference is small next to the cost of a re-dig.

Pros and Cons by Site Type

PROS
  • NP3 covers most municipal specs without over-ordering NP4.
  • Lighter units mean faster placement with smaller lifting gear.
  • Lower per-metre cost frees budget for bedding and backfill quality.
CONS
  • Under-specifying for future road upgrades means a costly re-dig later.
  • NP4 lead times run longer during peak monsoon-prep season.
  • Mixed-grade runs need careful marking so crews don't join grades incorrectly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can two different NP grades be jointed in the same run?

Yes, where cover depth changes along a route — but the transition point must be marked on the as-built drawing and jointed with a matched coupling.

How long does a batch take from order to dispatch?

Around 15 days including the fixed 14-day cure window, longer during peak monsoon-prep season.

Do you supply a batch test certificate with delivery?

Every delivery ships with a signed batch record covering load and dimension test results for that pour.
